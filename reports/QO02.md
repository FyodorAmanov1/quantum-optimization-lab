# QO02: Finite-shot Hubbard observables

## Question

Can local Pauli measurements of the optimized Hubbard dimer resolve electron localization, including the small double-occupancy decrease from U/t=6 to 8? How do energy precision and measurement grouping change the preparation cost?

At fixed total preparations, can a predetermined energy-focused allocation narrow the energy confidence interval without access to exact-state variances, and which other observables lose precision?

Can the same measurements certify a decrease at every adjacent pair of the seven-interaction scan, rather than only a preselected endpoint contrast?

## Method and exact references

Use the symmetric two-site Hubbard model H=K+UD at t=1 in the sector with one up-spin and one down-spin electron. The occupation basis is [3,6,9,12], with orbitals [site0-up, site0-down, site1-up, site1-down]; orbital 0 is the least significant bit. This is a defined effective model, without empirical materials data.

The existing three-angle real ansatz trains using exact expectations and three seeded BFGS starts. The legacy `vqe(interaction)` return is unchanged. Only evaluation uses finite shots; this is not finite-shot VQE training. After embedding the optimized state into 16 amplitudes, use

```text
K = -(XZXI + YZYI + IXZX + IYZY)/2
D = (2I - ZIII - IZII - IIZI - IIIZ + ZZII + IIZZ)/4
H = K + U D
```

Pauli words list orbital 0 first. One ZZZZ shot supplies all density terms jointly; four additional local settings measure hopping parities. X rotates with H and Y with H S-dagger. Independent multinomial draws from the rotated Born probabilities represent repeated projective measurements, with integer 16-outcome counts saved. Estimates are reconstructed from counts, not perturbed exact energies or sampled energy eigenvalues. This explicit partition follows the local grouping principle in [Verteletskyi, Yen and Izmaylov (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03358); it is not a general grouping optimizer.

Prepared-state expectations isolate measurement error. Independent diagonalization of the fermionic Hamiltonian supplies ground-state references. Analytic checks are E0=(U-sqrt(U^2+16))/2, D0=(1-U/sqrt(U^2+16))/2 and K0=-8/sqrt(U^2+16). See [Carrascal et al. (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02194) for dimer context. Tests compare the Pauli Hamiltonian with the separately implemented fermionic construction and check complex-state Y rotations.

The seven interactions are {0,0.5,1,2,4,6,8}; budgets are n={256,4096,65536} per grouped setting. The baseline measures six density Pauli terms and four hopping terms independently, with n/2 shots per term. Each strategy uses 5n preparations per interaction and supplies all three observables. The identity costs no shots. Grouped seed is 202600+10*i+b; baseline seed is 302600+10*i+b, where i and b index interaction and budget. Different budgets are independent, not nested. Counts, seeds and exact references for the original 42 runs are in [results/latest.json](../results/latest.json).

## Confidence intervals and costs

For observable estimate c+sum_g a_g mean(X_g), let R_g be the range length of the complete group value and n_g its shot count. Applying [Hoeffding (1963), Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830) directly to all independent weighted shots gives

```text
V_range = sum_g (a_g R_g)^2 / n_g
95% CI = estimate +/- sqrt(log(40) V_range / 2)
```

The primary source is also available as a [scan of the original paper](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf). Its unequal-range inequality applies to the independent summands a_g X_g/n_g. Within a density shot, Pauli terms are correlated and must be combined before applying the bound. Grouped D has range one in this sector, and each hopping contribution has range one. Ungrouped parity has range two, with D coefficients [-1/4,-1/4,-1/4,-1/4,1/4,1/4] and constant 1/2. The energy uses U times the density coefficients, including negative U.

These are distribution-free marginal intervals for prepared-state expectations. They can extend outside physical ranges and remain nonzero at zero sample variance. These original observable intervals have no simultaneous guarantee across observables, interactions or budgets; the separate density scan family below adds a simultaneous guarantee for its specified contrasts. The former group-wise union-bound intervals are retained in `union_bound_ci` as a diagnostic baseline; the direct weighted-shot formula is tighter without using exact probabilities or observed variance. The count-estimated SE retains group covariance. The exact sampling SD, computed from Born probabilities, is a separate simulator-only precision diagnostic; neither SE supplies the CI.

The preselected contrast is Delta=D(6)-D(8). Different interaction runs use independent preparations, so their V_range values add. A positive lower bound resolves the decrease at marginal 95% confidence; overlap of two individual CIs alone is not the test. Both interactions count toward the reported contrast cost, 10n preparations per strategy. This fixed comparison is not selected by searching for the most favorable measured change. Its CI covers the prepared-state difference; ground-state agreement depends on ansatz accuracy separately.

Equal shots in the original two baselines are fixed, not optimized. An additional grouped allocation is described below. Costs exclude exact training, physical state preparation circuits, gate compilation, basis-switching overhead, device errors and monetary costs. Multinomial aggregation reduces simulator storage, not the stated shot cost. At U=0, energy alone could omit the density setting, but it is retained to measure D as well.

## Exact binomial interval comparison

Does exploiting the binary density outcome resolve the preselected change with fewer preparations? In this sector each grouped Z measurement has D in {0,1}. An additional equal-tailed [Clopper-Pearson interval (1934)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/26.4.404) inverts binomial tails using beta quantiles from the pinned SciPy version. For k successes out of n, the endpoints are BetaQuantile(alpha/2; k,n-k+1) and BetaQuantile(1-alpha/2; k+1,n-k), with endpoints 0 at k=0 and 1 at k=n. Zero observed variance therefore does not imply zero uncertainty. This is a frequentist coverage guarantee, not a posterior probability about this realized interval.

For the independent-term baseline, six parity counts each receive failure probability alpha/6. Transform each success-probability interval to its signed contribution to D (reversing endpoints for negative coefficients), then sum with the identity offset. Bonferroni guarantees at least 95% coverage for this sum, without needing independence between component intervals. For the fixed contrast, compute each interaction's D interval at 97.5% confidence and subtract as [L6-H8,H6-L8]. The ungrouped contrast consequently divides the 5% error allowance across twelve parities. All intervals use counts alone, not exact probabilities. These are separate reported procedures: no data-dependent selection or intersection with Hoeffding is performed, and these original intervals claim no simultaneous scan coverage; the separate scan family below does.

At 4,096 shots per grouped setting, D(8)'s binomial interval is [0.0458598,0.0597677], versus its Hoeffding interval [0.0312699,0.0737106]. The grouped contrast now resolves the decrease using 40,960 combined preparations: sixteen times fewer than the largest tested budget where Hoeffding resolves it. This is an observed result on the fixed budget grid, not a minimum required cost or an estimated power curve. The same shots and point estimates are reused; no additional preparations were spent on interval computation.

The baseline is an instructive negative result: summing six simultaneous parity intervals is substantially more conservative than the weighted-shot Hoeffding bound. Its binomial contrast remains unresolved even at the largest budget, where Hoeffding resolves it. Exact binomial component intervals are not uniformly better for a weighted sum. Both procedures and their differing verdicts remain visible. All 21 grouped and 21 ungrouped individual D binomial intervals cover their exact prepared-state expectations in the recorded scan; this scan count does not establish coverage calibration. Energy and kinetic-energy CIs retain the original Hoeffding method.

The sector assumption is essential: with particle-number leakage, grouped D may take value two and this Bernoulli interval would be invalid. Input validation restricts the state to the specified sector; readout error, drift and correlated preparations are excluded. [The original paper scan](https://www.barestatistics.nl/uploads/1/1/7/9/11797954/clopper__pearson_1934.pdf) and [SciPy 1.15.2's exact-interval documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.15.2/reference/generated/scipy.stats._result_classes.BinomTestResult.proportion_ci.html) were verified; the publisher DOI redirects to an access-restricted page.

## Predetermined energy-focused shot allocation

The third strategy retains the same five grouped settings and total preparation budgets, changing only their shot counts. For energy the coefficient-weighted ranges are r=(abs(U),1,1,1,1). With fixed total N, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

```text
sum_g r_g^2 / n_g >= (sum_g r_g)^2 / N
```

The continuous equality allocation is n_g proportional to r_g. This is a direct optimization of the range proxy in the [Hoeffding bound](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf), not a claim about optimal variance or a new measurement algorithm. The implemented rule reserves two shots per setting, divides the remaining N-10 proportional to r, floors the counts and assigns the leftover shots by largest fractional remainder, breaking ties in setting order. Reservation and rounding make this an approximation to the continuous solution; exact integer optimality is not claimed. It needs only U and the total budget. It uses no exact wavefunction, variance, pilot measurements or adaptive stopping. Every reserved shot is counted.

`measure_hubbard(..., shot_allocation=[...])` accepts fixed unequal counts for either grouping while retaining the original scalar `shots` API. Each setting mean divides by its own n_g. Its sample variance contribution divides by n_g(n_g-1), and both Hoeffding and the diagnostic exact sampling variance use the actual per-setting counts. Density binomial intervals also use their actual count totals. A uniform allocation reproduces the scalar API exactly. `shot_allocation` records every count; `shots_per_setting` is null for unequal allocations. Settings remain ordered ZZZZ, XZXI, YZYI, IXZX, IYZY for grouped runs. The independent-term order is ZIII, IZII, IIZI, IIIZ, ZZII, IIZZ followed by the four hopping settings.

There are 21 additional runs with seed 402600+10*i+b, independent of both baselines; all 63 measurement runs and their raw counts are saved. The original contrast study and its negative findings remain unchanged. At U/t=8:

All 63 additional marginal intervals cover their prepared-state references, which is descriptive rather than a coverage calibration. ## Simultaneous density scan: all adjacent decreases

The fixed family comprises D(0)-D(0.5), D(0.5)-D(1), D(1)-D(2), D(2)-D(4), D(4)-D(6) and D(6)-D(8). The new `density_scan_intervals` calculation reuses the existing measured counts. It does not train again, spend additional shots, select promising comparisons or intersect procedures. References are the differences of the exact prepared-state expectations and independently diagonalized ground-state values already recorded for each interaction.

For a family failure allowance alpha=0.05, one simultaneous procedure applies the weighted-shot Hoeffding formula to each difference with alpha/6, so its radius is sqrt(log(240)*(V_left+V_right)/2). A separate binomial procedure constructs each interaction's D interval with confidence 1-alpha/7, then subtracts adjacent endpoints: [L_i-H_(i+1), H_i-L_(i+1)]. For independent Pauli terms, each interaction further divides its alpha/7 across six parity intervals. This uses the existing [Clopper-Pearson binomial limits](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/26.4.404) and [Hoeffding inequality](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf); both primary records were verified. The finite union bound gives failure probability at most seven times alpha/7 (or six times alpha/6). This elementary aggregation is the implementation's derivation, not a new result attributed to those papers.

Adjacent contrasts share their middle interaction and are correlated. No independence between contrasts is assumed. Hoeffding does require independent preparations within and between interaction runs; the saved runs use distinct seeds. All seven interactions' preparations are counted once, rather than charging twelve interaction evaluations for six adjacent pairs. Each procedure's simultaneous guarantee applies separately to one strategy and budget, to prepared-state differences only. It is not joint across procedures, strategies or budgets, and says nothing between the sampled interaction strengths.

Thus equal grouping resolves the full sampled scan under the binomial procedure at the intermediate budget. Its weakest bound is D(6)-D(8): estimate 0.0288086, simultaneous binomial interval [0.00765920,0.04988081], exact ground-state difference 0.03118845. The separate Hoeffding family leaves this pair unresolved with [-0.00777073,0.06538791]. At the largest budget both equal-shot strategies resolve all six under Hoeffding, while the independent-term binomial procedure still fails on the last pair. Neither interval procedure uniformly dominates the other.

The energy-focused rule fails to resolve the first pair at every tested budget. Even at 2,293,760 preparations its D(0)-D(0.5) binomial interval is [-0.43886674,0.57155868], since the U=0 density count remains two. A large total budget cannot repair an allocation that withholds measurements from an observable needed for the decision. The intermediate-budget equal grouping result uses the same shots as the original marginal analysis, with a more demanding simultaneous criterion. It identifies a successful point on this fixed budget grid, not a minimum sufficient budget or an estimated detection probability.

## Limitations and interview understanding

This is a tiny classically simulated symmetric dimer, without device noise, finite-shot training, empirical materials validation or quantum advantage. Exact ansatz expressivity makes prepared-state bias tiny here; confidence intervals do not bound that bias or model error. Broad localization is cheaper to observe than a small late change or precise energy. Variance-adaptive bounds and allocations with explicit precision constraints on multiple observables remain future work.

The author should derive the Pauli mapping and bit order, reconstruct D and K from counts, and explain why density terms measured together retain covariance. They should derive the weighted-shot Hoeffding formula by counting independent summands, contrast it with the older union bound, distinguish sample SE from confidence bounds, and add both interaction budgets for a contrast. They should explain why a marginal interval may miss, why an unresolved difference is not evidence of no change, why U amplifies energy uncertainty, and why noisy energy estimates can lie below the exact ground value.

For an interview, also explain why D is Bernoulli only in the chosen sector, how the 5% error allowance is divided across component intervals, why a sum of exact binomial intervals can be wider than Hoeffding, and why a smaller interval on one fixed scan is not a demonstrated detection probability.

Derive the continuous coefficient-range allocation using Cauchy-Schwarz, account for reserved and rounded shots, and explain why narrower Hoeffding energy bounds can coexist with worse actual variance or a worse seeded scan error. Explain why improving energy precision alone does not guarantee useful measurements of the other physical observables.

For the scan extension, the author should explain why six marginal 95% contrast intervals do not guarantee 95% coverage of the whole scan, why neighbouring differences are correlated, how the union bound handles this dependence, and why preparation cost counts each interaction once. Distinguish a resolved finite grid from monotonicity of an entire continuous curve, and explain the retained failure of energy-only shot allocation for density decisions.
