# QO02: Finite-shot Hubbard observables

## Question

Can local Pauli measurements of the existing optimized two-site Hubbard state resolve the suppression of double occupancy as repulsion increases, and what shot cost is needed to estimate energy as well?

## Method and exact baselines

The model is H = K + U D at hopping t=1, with K the spin-preserving hopping operator and D = sum_i n_i_up n_i_down. The four orbitals are site0-up, site0-down, site1-up, site1-down. The sector has one electron of each spin, with integer occupation basis [3,6,9,12]. Orbital 0 is the least significant bit; Pauli words below list orbital 0 first. This is a defined effective-electron model, with no empirical materials inputs.

The existing three-angle real ansatz and three seeded BFGS starts still train on exact statevector expectations. The public `vqe(interaction)` four-metric return is preserved; `vqe_state` additionally exposes the prepared state. Only the subsequent observable evaluation uses finite shots. This distinction prevents attributing measurement cost to a finite-shot optimizer that was not run.

Embed that state in the 16-amplitude occupation register. The Jordan-Wigner parity signs give:

```text
K = -(XZXI + YZYI + IXZX + IYZY)/2
D = (2I - ZIII - IZII - IIZI - IIIZ + ZZII + IIZZ)/4
H = K + U D
```

One ZZZZ setting measures all six nonidentity density terms from the same bitstring. Each hopping word uses its own product setting. X measurements rotate with H, Y with H S-dagger, and Z with identity. Each setting draws a multinomial count vector from the rotated Born probabilities, equivalent to independent repeated projective measurements. Estimates are reconstructed from those integer counts. This samples neither energy eigenvalues nor Gaussian perturbations of exact answers. The implementation uses the qubit-wise commuting grouping principle of [Verteletskyi, Yen and Izmaylov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03358), with an explicit partition rather than an optimizer for arbitrary Hamiltonians.

The exact baselines are separate: prepared-state expectations identify measurement error, while independent diagonalization of the fermionic Hamiltonian identifies the ground-state reference. Analytic cross-checks are E0=(U-sqrt(U²+16))/2, D0=(1-U/sqrt(U²+16))/2, and K0=-8/sqrt(U²+16). Dimer context and exact solvability are discussed by [Carrascal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02194). Tests verify the Pauli matrices against the separately constructed fermionic Hamiltonian, not just against the sampling implementation.

Use U/t in {0,0.5,1,2,4,6,8} and n in {256,4096,65536} shots per setting. Run seed is 202600 + 10*i + b, with i the interaction index and b the budget index. Runs at different budgets are independent, not nested. All 21 runs, 16-outcome counts for each setting, seeds, estimates, standard errors and exact references are saved in [results/latest.json](../results/latest.json).

## Uncertainty and costs

For one observable, let a_g be its coefficients on the active group estimates and k the number of active groups. Each unweighted group sample has range length one: D in {0,1} in this input sector, and each hopping contribution in {-1/2,+1/2}. With alpha=0.05, use the interval

```text
estimate ± sum_g |a_g| sqrt(log(2*k/alpha)/(2*n)).
```

[Hoeffding's bounded-mean inequality](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830), with failure probability alpha/k for each group, and a union bound prove at least 95% coverage for each prepared-state observable. The energy coefficients are [U,1,1,1,1]; the U=0 density contribution is excluded from the active groups. These bounds are conservative and marginal, with no simultaneous claim across observables, interactions or budgets. They remain nonzero even with zero observed variance and can extend outside physical ranges. The displayed standard error instead uses the unbiased sample variance of each complete group value, combined across independent settings; it retains within-group density covariance and is not used to construct the CI.

Five settings require 5*n independent state preparations: 1,280, 20,480 or 327,680 per interaction. Measuring each of the ten nonidentity Pauli terms separately at n shots would require 10*n preparations when all terms are needed. This is a setting-count baseline, not a measured equal-total-shot precision comparison. At U=0 the energy alone needs only four settings; the fifth still supplies the requested D observable. Identity terms cost no shots. There is no claim that five settings is a globally optimal measurement scheme. Training, preparation circuits, compiled gates, device errors, monetary cost and runtime advantage are excluded. Multinomial aggregation saves simulator storage but represents the stated number of shots.

## Actual findings

Across the seven interactions, errors relative to exact ground expectations are:

| Shots/setting | Total shots/interaction | Energy RMSE / t | D RMSE | K RMSE / t | D CI half-width | Energy CI half-width range / t |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 256 | 1,280 | 0.0614524 | 0.0215317 | 0.0341268 | 0.0848813 | 0.398245–1.220718 |
| 4,096 | 20,480 | 0.00668117 | 0.00316496 | 0.00826840 | 0.0212203 | 0.0995613–0.305179 |
| 65,536 | 327,680 | 0.00438695 | 0.00113795 | 0.000730548 | 0.00530508 | 0.0248903–0.0762949 |

At 4,096 shots/setting, D(U=0)=0.4997559 with CI [0.4785355,0.5209762], while D(U=8)=0.0524902 with CI [0.0312699,0.0737106]. Their exact ground values are 0.5 and 0.0527864. The endpoint intervals are disjoint even at 256 shots/setting: the broad localization trend is visible. However, the U=6 and U=8 D intervals overlap at 4,096 shots/setting, so this conservative display does not resolve every adjacent change.

Energy at U=8 and 4,096 shots/setting is -0.4648438 t versus exact -0.4721360 t, with CI [-0.7700232,-0.1596643] t. The observed error is small but the bound is wide: multiplication of the density uncertainty by U makes high-repulsion energy certification expensive. Even at 65,536 shots/setting, the U=8 energy half-width is 0.0762949 t. The sampled energy there is -0.4771118 t, below the exact ground energy. This is permitted for a noisy estimator and is not a variational-bound violation by the prepared state.

All 63 recorded marginal intervals contain their respective exact ground references; this is an observation on a small conservative scan, not a measured 95% coverage calibration. Prepared-state bias is tiny here because the real ansatz is fully expressive in the four-dimensional sector. At U=0 the sampled K and energy happen to be exactly -2 with zero sample SE; their Hoeffding intervals correctly remain nonzero. Individual errors need not decrease monotonically with shots or follow the interval contraction exactly. The interval widths shrink by four for each sixteen-fold increase in n.

## Limitations and interview understanding

These are ideal simulated Born measurements after exact training, without device noise or finite-shot optimization. The model is a tiny symmetric Hubbard dimer, not ab initio chemistry, a battery property or evidence of quantum advantage. Confidence intervals describe measurement uncertainty of the prepared state; they do not bound ansatz bias or model discrepancy. The chosen bound is intentionally conservative; variance-adaptive intervals and shot allocation could reduce its width but were not studied. A reduction in measurement settings is not proof of better accuracy at matched total shots.

The author should be able to derive the parity strings and orbital ordering, reconstruct D and K from counts, explain shared-shot covariance, and distinguish standard error from a finite-sample confidence bound. They should derive the union-bound formula and explain why U amplifies the energy uncertainty. They should distinguish exact training, finite-shot evaluation, prepared-state references and ground-state references, explain sampled energies below the exact ground energy, and retain the negative result that a clear localization trend does not certify a precise energy or every adjacent change.
