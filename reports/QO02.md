# QO02: Finite-shot Hubbard observables

## Question

Can local Pauli measurements of the optimized Hubbard dimer resolve electron localization, including the small double-occupancy decrease from U/t=6 to 8? How do energy precision and measurement grouping change the preparation cost?

## Method and exact references

Use the symmetric two-site Hubbard model H=K+UD at t=1 in the sector with one up-spin and one down-spin electron. The occupation basis is [3,6,9,12], with orbitals [site0-up, site0-down, site1-up, site1-down]; orbital 0 is the least significant bit. This is a defined effective model, without empirical materials data.

The existing three-angle real ansatz trains using exact expectations and three seeded BFGS starts. The legacy `vqe(interaction)` return is unchanged. Only evaluation uses finite shots; this is not finite-shot VQE training. After embedding the optimized state into 16 amplitudes, use

```text
K = -(XZXI + YZYI + IXZX + IYZY)/2
D = (2I - ZIII - IZII - IIZI - IIIZ + ZZII + IIZZ)/4
H = K + U D
```

Pauli words list orbital 0 first. One ZZZZ shot supplies all density terms jointly; four additional local settings measure hopping parities. X rotates with H and Y with H S-dagger. Independent multinomial draws from the rotated Born probabilities represent repeated projective measurements, with integer 16-outcome counts saved. Estimates are reconstructed from counts, not perturbed exact energies or sampled energy eigenvalues. This explicit partition follows the local grouping principle in [Verteletskyi, Yen and Izmaylov (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03358); it is not a general grouping optimizer.

Prepared-state expectations isolate measurement error. Independent diagonalization of the fermionic Hamiltonian supplies ground-state references. Analytic checks are E0=(U-sqrt(U^2+16))/2, D0=(1-U/sqrt(U^2+16))/2 and K0=-8/sqrt(U^2+16). See [Carrascal et al. (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02194) for dimer context. Tests compare the Pauli Hamiltonian with the separately implemented fermionic construction and check complex-state Y rotations.

The seven interactions are {0,0.5,1,2,4,6,8}; budgets are n={256,4096,65536} per grouped setting. The baseline measures six density Pauli terms and four hopping terms independently, with n/2 shots per term. Each strategy uses 5n preparations per interaction and supplies all three observables. The identity costs no shots. Grouped seed is 202600+10*i+b; baseline seed is 302600+10*i+b, where i and b index interaction and budget. Different budgets are independent, not nested. Counts, seeds and exact references for all 42 runs are in [results/latest.json](../results/latest.json).

## Confidence intervals and costs

For observable estimate c+sum_g a_g mean(X_g), let R_g be the range length of the complete group value and n_g its shot count. Applying [Hoeffding (1963), Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830) directly to all independent weighted shots gives

```text
V_range = sum_g (a_g R_g)^2 / n_g
95% CI = estimate +/- sqrt(log(40) V_range / 2)
```

The primary source is also available as a [scan of the original paper](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf). Its unequal-range inequality applies to the independent summands a_g X_g/n_g. Within a density shot, Pauli terms are correlated and must be combined before applying the bound. Grouped D has range one in this sector, and each hopping contribution has range one. Ungrouped parity has range two, with D coefficients [-1/4,-1/4,-1/4,-1/4,1/4,1/4] and constant 1/2. The energy uses U times the density coefficients, including negative U.

These are distribution-free marginal intervals for prepared-state expectations. They can extend outside physical ranges and remain nonzero at zero sample variance. No simultaneous guarantee across observables, interactions or budgets is claimed. The former group-wise union-bound intervals are retained in `union_bound_ci` as a diagnostic baseline; the direct weighted-shot formula is tighter without using exact probabilities or observed variance. The count-estimated SE retains group covariance. The exact sampling SD, computed from Born probabilities, is a separate simulator-only precision diagnostic; neither SE supplies the CI.

The preselected contrast is Delta=D(6)-D(8). Different interaction runs use independent preparations, so their V_range values add. A positive lower bound resolves the decrease at marginal 95% confidence; overlap of two individual CIs alone is not the test. Both interactions count toward the reported contrast cost, 10n preparations per strategy. This fixed comparison is not selected by searching for the most favorable measured change. Its CI covers the prepared-state difference; ground-state agreement depends on ansatz accuracy separately.

Equal shots within each strategy are fixed, not optimized. Costs exclude exact training, physical state preparation circuits, gate compilation, basis-switching overhead, device errors and monetary costs. Multinomial aggregation reduces simulator storage, not the stated shot cost. At U=0, energy alone could omit the density setting, but it is retained to measure D as well.

Marginal 95% coverage permits occasional misses; this small correlated collection of observables is not a coverage calibration. Separate repeated-seed tests check sampling means and variances, and an exact binomial calculation checks density-interval coverage at small shot counts.

At U=0, energy samples can be exactly -2 with zero sample SE while the confidence bound remains positive. At U=8 and the largest budget, sampled energy is -0.4771118 t, below the exact ground energy: a sampling fluctuation, not a variational violation by the prepared state. Observed errors need not decrease monotonically with shots. Interval half-widths decrease by four for each sixteen-fold increase in shots.

## Exact binomial interval comparison

Does exploiting the binary density outcome resolve the preselected change with fewer preparations? In this sector each grouped Z measurement has D in {0,1}. An additional equal-tailed [Clopper-Pearson interval (1934)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/26.4.404) inverts binomial tails using beta quantiles from the pinned SciPy version. For k successes out of n, the endpoints are BetaQuantile(alpha/2; k,n-k+1) and BetaQuantile(1-alpha/2; k+1,n-k), with endpoints 0 at k=0 and 1 at k=n. Zero observed variance therefore does not imply zero uncertainty. This is a frequentist coverage guarantee, not a posterior probability about this realized interval.

For the independent-term baseline, six parity counts each receive failure probability alpha/6. Transform each success-probability interval to its signed contribution to D (reversing endpoints for negative coefficients), then sum with the identity offset. Bonferroni guarantees at least 95% coverage for this sum, without needing independence between component intervals. For the fixed contrast, compute each interaction's D interval at 97.5% confidence and subtract as [L6-H8,H6-L8]. The ungrouped contrast consequently divides the 5% error allowance across twelve parities. All intervals use counts alone, not exact probabilities. These are separate reported procedures: no data-dependent selection or intersection with Hoeffding is performed, and no simultaneous scan coverage is claimed.

At 4,096 shots per grouped setting, D(8)'s binomial interval is [0.0458598,0.0597677], versus its Hoeffding interval [0.0312699,0.0737106]. The grouped contrast now resolves the decrease using 40,960 combined preparations: sixteen times fewer than the largest tested budget where Hoeffding resolves it. This is an observed result on the fixed budget grid, not a minimum required cost or an estimated power curve. The same shots and point estimates are reused; no additional preparations were spent on interval computation.

The baseline is an instructive negative result: summing six simultaneous parity intervals is substantially more conservative than the weighted-shot Hoeffding bound. Its binomial contrast remains unresolved even at the largest budget, where Hoeffding resolves it. Exact binomial component intervals are not uniformly better for a weighted sum. Both procedures and their differing verdicts remain visible. All 21 grouped and 21 ungrouped individual D binomial intervals cover their exact prepared-state expectations in the recorded scan; this scan count does not establish coverage calibration. Energy and kinetic-energy CIs retain the original Hoeffding method.

The sector assumption is essential: with particle-number leakage, grouped D may take value two and this Bernoulli interval would be invalid. Input validation restricts the state to the specified sector; readout error, drift and correlated preparations are excluded. [The original paper scan](https://www.barestatistics.nl/uploads/1/1/7/9/11797954/clopper__pearson_1934.pdf) and [SciPy 1.15.2's exact-interval documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.15.2/reference/generated/scipy.stats._result_classes.BinomTestResult.proportion_ci.html) were verified; the publisher DOI redirects to an access-restricted page.

## Limitations and interview understanding

This is a tiny classically simulated symmetric dimer, without device noise, finite-shot training, empirical materials validation or quantum advantage. Exact ansatz expressivity makes prepared-state bias tiny here; confidence intervals do not bound that bias or model error. Broad localization is cheaper to observe than a small late change or precise energy. Variance-adaptive bounds and optimized shot allocation remain future work.

The author should derive the Pauli mapping and bit order, reconstruct D and K from counts, and explain why density terms measured together retain covariance. They should derive the weighted-shot Hoeffding formula by counting independent summands, contrast it with the older union bound, distinguish sample SE from confidence bounds, and add both interaction budgets for a contrast. They should explain why a marginal interval may miss, why an unresolved difference is not evidence of no change, why U amplifies energy uncertainty, and why noisy energy estimates can lie below the exact ground value.

For an interview, also explain why D is Bernoulli only in the chosen sector, how the 5% error allowance is divided across component intervals, why a sum of exact binomial intervals can be wider than Hoeffding, and why a smaller interval on one fixed scan is not a demonstrated detection probability.
